Key Insights

1. Survival was not equally distributed among all passengers. A larger number
   of passengers did not survive compared with those who survived.

2. Gender had a strong relationship with survival. Female passengers had a
   considerably higher survival rate than male passengers.

3. Passenger class was associated with survival. Passengers from higher
   classes generally had better survival outcomes than passengers from
   lower classes.

4. The age distribution shows that Titanic passengers belonged to different
   age groups, with many passengers concentrated in the young-adult and
   middle-age ranges.

5. Fare values were highly varied. Some passengers paid substantially higher
   fares than most other passengers, which can be identified as potential
   outliers.

6. The correlation analysis shows relationships among numerical variables
   such as passenger class, fare, age, and survival.

7. The EDA shows that passenger characteristics such as gender, class,
   age, and fare can be useful variables for understanding survival patterns.

Conclusion

Exploratory Data Analysis was performed on the cleaned Titanic dataset using
Python. Important statistics were calculated and several visualizations were
created to understand passenger characteristics and survival patterns.
The analysis identified relationships between survival and variables such as
gender and passenger class. Age and fare distributions were also analyzed,
and potential anomalies were identified using boxplots and the IQR method.
Overall, EDA helped identify important patterns, trends, and relationships
within the Titanic dataset.
